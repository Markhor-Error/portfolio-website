How the calculation works

Three steps, and the tool shows you all of them.

  1. Turn each round into a score differential. (113 ÷ slope) × (score − course rating). This strips out how hard the course was, so a 92 on a brutal layout and an 86 on an easy one become comparable numbers.
  2. Take your lowest differentials. How many depends on how many rounds you have posted — see the table below. Your handicap reflects your potential, which is why it uses your better rounds rather than your average.
  3. Average them and apply any adjustment. With fewer than five scores an extra adjustment is subtracted, because a small sample is less reliable.

What this tool does not do

It does not apply net double bogey adjustment to your gross score, the playing conditions calculation, or the soft and hard caps that limit how fast an index can rise. An official index from your golf association will include those. Treat this as a close estimate and a way to understand the method, not as a replacement.

Common questions

How is a handicap index actually calculated?

Each round is converted into a score differential using the formula (113 divided by the slope rating) multiplied by (adjusted gross score minus course rating). Your index is then the average of a set number of your lowest differentials — eight of your last twenty once you have twenty scores, and fewer when you have fewer rounds.

Why 113?

113 is the slope rating of a course of standard playing difficulty. Dividing by the actual slope and multiplying by 113 normalises your score so a round on a hard course and a round on an easy one can be compared fairly.

What is the difference between course rating and slope rating?

Course rating is the score a scratch golfer is expected to shoot, so it is a number close to par. Slope rating measures how much harder the course plays for a bogey golfer than for a scratch golfer, on a scale from 55 to 155 where 113 is average. Course rating tells you how hard it is; slope tells you how much harder it gets as you get worse.

Is this the same as an official handicap?

No. This calculator applies the standard method to the scores you enter, but an official index comes from an authorised golf association using verified scores, and it also applies adjustments this tool does not model — including net double bogey caps, playing conditions calculation, and the soft and hard cap on upward movement. Use this as an estimate, not as a substitute for a real index.

How many scores do I need?

A minimum of three eighteen-hole scores. With three you take the lowest differential and subtract 2.0; the adjustment shrinks as you post more rounds and disappears once you have five. The full method of averaging the best eight starts at twenty scores.
