How the two ball flight laws work

Nearly every curved golf shot comes down to two numbers at impact: where the face is pointing, and where the club is travelling. Understanding the relationship between them is the difference between fixing a slice and chasing it for a decade.

The part most golfers get backwards

If your ball starts left and slices right, the instinct is to aim further left. That makes the path more out-to-in, which makes the slice worse. The start line is not the problem — the gap between face and path is.

All nine ball flights

Three start directions multiplied by three curve directions gives nine possible flights. Every shot you have ever hit is one of them.

The nine ball flights and their impact conditions
FlightStartsCurvesFace and path at impact
Pull slice Left Right The face was left of your target at impact, and the path was even further left of the face. That combination starts the ball left and tilts the spin axis hard to the right.
Straight slice Straight Right The face was close to square to your target, but the path was left of the face. The start line looks fine, which is why this one is so frustrating.
Push slice Right Right The face was open to your target, and the path was left of that open face. This is the biggest miss in golf — it starts right and keeps going right.
Push hook Right Left The face was right of your target, and the path was even further right of the face. The ball starts right, then turns hard left.
Straight hook Straight Left The face was near square to your target, with the path to the right of it. The ball sets off on line and then dives left.
Pull hook Left Left The face was left of your target and the path was right of the face — a closed face with an in-to-out path. The two-way miss golfers fear most.
Straight pull Left No curve Face and path were matched, but both were aimed left of your target. Mechanically this is a good strike sent to the wrong address.
Straight push Right No curve Face and path matched each other but both pointed right of the target. A well-struck shot with the wrong start line.
Straight Straight No curve Face and path were both square to the target and matched each other. There is nothing to fix here.

Directions above are for a right-handed golfer. The tool mirrors them if you select left-handed.

Common questions

Does the club path or the club face decide where the ball starts?

The face is the dominant influence on start direction. Under the modern ball flight laws the ball begins its flight close to where the face points at impact, and the face has more influence the less loft the club has. The older idea that path alone sets the start line has been superseded by launch monitor data.

What actually makes a golf ball curve?

The difference between the club face angle and the club path, often called face-to-path. If the path is right of where the face points, the spin axis tilts left and the ball curves left. If the path is left of the face, the ball curves right. A face and path that match produce a straight ball.

Why does my ball start left and curve right?

That is a pull slice, and it is the most common miss in amateur golf. It means the face was left of your target but the path was even further left of the face. The path is usually the root cause, and alignment is usually the root cause of the path.

Is this diagnostic accurate without a launch monitor?

It infers impact conditions from the shot shape you observe, which is reliable for identifying which pattern you have. It cannot give you numbers. If you want measured face angle and path values you need a launch monitor, but you do not need one to work out which of the nine ball flights you are hitting.
