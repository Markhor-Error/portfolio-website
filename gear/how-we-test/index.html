Methodology

How We Test and Rank Golf Equipment

Most gear roundups imply a level of testing that never happened. This page tells you exactly what evidence sits behind each recommendation on this site, including where that evidence is thin.

The short version

Every product recommendation on Going Eagle carries an evidence label saying how we formed it. Nothing is described as tested unless someone physically hit it. Where our evidence is research only, the page says so in the same size type as everything else.

Our evidence labels

You will see one of these three labels on every product we recommend. They are not marketing language — they describe what we actually did.

LabelWhat it meansWhat it does not mean
Hands-on Someone on the team has hit, used or built with this product themselves, over more than a single session. Not a controlled lab test. We are not a robot-testing facility and will not pretend to be one.
Fitted data Our assessment draws on launch monitor numbers gathered during a fitting or a range session with this model. Not a claim that the numbers generalise to your swing. Fitting data is specific to the person who generated it.
Research-based We have not used it. The assessment comes from manufacturer specifications, independent test data we cite, and owner feedback. Not a personal endorsement. We say plainly when we have not touched something.

What we actually assess

For clubs, the questions that matter to a mid-handicap golfer are not the ones that matter to a tour player. We weight our assessments accordingly:

  • Forgiveness on mis-hits — how much you lose on a strike off the centre, which for most amateurs matters more than peak distance.
  • Launch and spin behaviour — whether the club suits a moderate swing speed or needs speed to work.
  • Adjustability — whether you can change the setup as your swing changes, which affects how long the club stays useful.
  • Real cost of ownership — street price rather than list price, and whether last year's model does the same job for less.
  • Who it is wrong for — every recommendation names the golfer who should not buy it.

The limits of what we do

We would rather state these plainly than have you discover them:

  • We do not run robot testing. Any comparison involving a human swing carries variance we cannot fully eliminate, and we will not present a small sample as if it were definitive.
  • We cannot test every product in a category. A roundup covers the models that matter to the reader, not every model on the market, and we say which ones we skipped.
  • Equipment performance is individual. A driver that adds fifteen yards for one golfer can cost another five. This is why almost every guide here ends up recommending a fitting over a specific product.
  • Prices move constantly. We do not print prices we cannot keep current — we describe price tiers instead and point you at the retailer for the live figure.

How commission affects our rankings

It does not, and here is the specific commitment: we rank on merit and then attach links, never the other way round. Some of the brands we rate most highly pay low commission rates or run no affiliate program at all. When the best answer is a product we earn nothing from, or the answer is “keep the clubs you already own”, that is what the page says.

Affiliate links are disclosed above the first one on every page, marked appropriately in the HTML, and explained in full on our affiliate disclosure.

Review before publication

Gear advice is reviewed by Mike Evans, Golf Coach — 6 years of coaching experience, before it is published — the same process described in our editorial policy. A coach who fits clubs to real golfers is a useful check on whether a recommendation would actually help the person reading it.

Tell us when we are wrong

If you own something we have reviewed and your experience contradicts ours, we want to hear it. Get in touch — reader corrections have changed recommendations on this site before, and we would rather be corrected than consistent.
