The hook is the miss that arrives once you have got good enough to stop slicing. It goes a long
way, which is the problem — a slice that leaks right costs you twenty yards, and a hook that turns
over costs you the hole, because it is still running when it reaches the trees.

It also gets confused with a pull constantly, and the two need opposite fixes. So before anything
else, work out which one you are hitting.

## What a hook actually is

The same two rules that govern every curved shot:

1. **The club face** controls where the ball starts.
2. **The difference between face and path** controls how much, and which way, it curves.

So the definition is precise:

> A hook is a club face that is **closed relative to the swing path** at impact. Not closed to the
> target — closed to the path.

A face pointing 4° right of target with a path 4° right produces a dead straight push with no
curve. Move the path to 8° right without moving the face and you have a shot that starts right and
turns hard left. The face never changed. The relationship did.

## Hook or pull? Watch the start line

Three shots go left and only one is a hook.

| Starts | Bends | What it is | Root cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right | Left | **Push hook** — the classic | Path well right of the face |
| Straight | Left | **Straight hook** | Path right, face happens to match target |
| Left | Left | **Pull hook** — the destructive one | Face closed to target, path right of it |
| Left | None | Pull, not a hook | Alignment or ball position |
| Right | None | Push, not a hook | Alignment or ball position |

If your ball starts left and flies dead straight, you have a pull and an aim problem. Weakening
your grip will make it worse, because the grip is not what is wrong.

Watch five shots and note the **start** direction before the curve. The
[ball flight diagnostic](/tools/ball-flight-diagnostic/) covers all nine flights if you want to
check your reading.

## Which of the two causes is yours

Both produce a hook. They need different work.

**Hit five mid irons at a specific target and note where each one starts.**

- **If they start right of target**, your path is running too far to the right and the face is
  closing to catch up. This is the *path-driven hook*, and it is common in golfers who have been
  told for years to swing more from the inside.
- **If they start left of target**, your face is genuinely closed at impact and the path is left
  too. This is the *face-driven hook*, and it is the one that produces the low, hard, unplayable
  pull hook.

Use an iron, not a driver. Driver flight exaggerates everything.

## Fixing the face-driven hook

Your face arrives closed. Work down this list and stop when the ball flight changes.

### 1. Look at your grip

Look down at address. If you can see four knuckles on your lead hand, or the V between your trail
thumb and forefinger points outside your trail shoulder, the face has a head start on closing.

Rotate both hands slightly toward the target until you see two to three knuckles. It will feel
weak and the face will feel open. That feeling is the change, and it settles within a bucket of
balls. Change one hand at a time if the whole thing feels unmanageable.

### 2. Check what your trail hand is doing through impact

A hook is often a trail hand that takes over and rolls the face shut. The feel that helps is
holding the back of the lead hand pointing at the target for longer past impact — not blocking,
just not flipping.

This is a feel, not a position. If it produces a shot that starts right and stays right, you have
overdone it; back off until the ball starts on line.

### 3. Keep the body turning

This is the one grip advice cannot fix, and it is the real cause more often than the grip is.

If your hips and chest stop rotating through impact, your arms and hands arrive on their own — and
the quickest thing they can do is close the face. The swing feels powerful and the ball goes left.

The test: hit shots and pay attention to whether your chest is still turning after contact. If
your finish has you facing the ball rather than the target, the body stopped. That is your hook.

## Fixing the path-driven hook

Your path swings too far right for the face to match. Usually the result of overcooking an
in-to-out fix.

### 1. Check your alignment first

Lay a club on the ground at your target and set up to it. Golfers who have worked hard on swinging
from the inside frequently end up aimed well right without knowing it, and then swing along that
line. The path is not broken; it is doing what your aim asked.

### 2. Stop chasing "more inside"

The trap mirrors the slicer's. The ball goes left, so you try to swing further from the inside to
hold it off, which pushes the path further right, which widens the gap between path and face,
which makes it hook more.

If you have been adding "inside" every season and hooking more each year, this loop is why.

### 3. Neutralise the path with a gate

Put a headcover just outside the ball and another just inside and behind it, so an excessively
in-to-out swing clips the inner one. Make slow swings that miss both. You are not trying to swing
left; you are trying to stop swinging so far right.

### 4. Let the body lead, not the arms

A path that runs hard right is often arms dropping behind the body while the chest stalls. Feel
the chest starting the downswing rotation with the arms following, rather than the arms dropping
into a slot and waiting.

## The order to work in

Stop when the ball flight changes.

1. **Alignment** — free, instant, and the cause more often than expected.
2. **Grip** — one adjustment, twenty balls, then judge.
3. **Body rotation through impact** — the fix that holds up under pressure.
4. **Path work with a gate** — only now, and only with feedback.

The order is deliberate. Every item above the one you need can create the fault you are trying to
fix lower down. Change your path while a strong grip is still closing the face and you will just
hook it from a new direction.

## What to expect

You are removing a compensation rather than adding a skill, and that has a specific consequence:
**the first correct swings will feel like blocks and some will go right.** Your hands have been
saving a face that arrived open relative to where you were swinging, and when you stop them, the
ball goes where the face was actually pointing all along.

That phase is the change working, not failing. Give it a session before you judge it, and judge it
on start line first and curve second.

If your ball starts curving the other way, our [slice guide](/fixes/slice/) covers the opposite
fault — and the fact that you can now hit both means your face is moving, which is a better place
to be than stuck at one extreme.
