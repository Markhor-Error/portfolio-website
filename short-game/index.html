Short Game

Short Game: Where Amateur Golfers Lose the Most Strokes

Inside 100 yards is the cheapest place in golf to find strokes. It needs no swing speed, no new equipment and no athletic ability — which is exactly why so few golfers practise it.

ME

Written by Going Eagle Editorial Team · Reviewed by Mike Evans, Golf Coach — 6 years of coaching experience

Why the short game pays back faster than anything else

A driver that goes fifteen yards further saves you a fraction of a stroke per round. Being able to get up and down instead of taking three to get down saves you a full stroke every time it happens, and for most mid-handicap golfers it happens several times a round.

The short game also degrades least with age and requires the least physical capability. A 68-year-old with a good short game will beat a strong 25-year-old without one, reliably, forever.

The honest reason it gets neglected

Range practice feels better. Hitting fifty drivers produces a handful of shots that feel superb, and that feeling is the reward. Hitting fifty chips produces no such moments — the good ones are unremarkable by definition. The practice that improves your score is frequently the practice that is least satisfying to do.

A useful test

Drop ten balls around a practice green at varying lies and try to get each one up and down. Count the successes. Most golfers who describe their short game as "not bad" score two or three out of ten, and are genuinely surprised by it.
