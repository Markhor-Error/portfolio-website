Home Golf: Building a Practice Setup That Actually Gets Used

A home setup is the difference between practising twice a month and practising four times a week. It is also the easiest place in golf to waste several thousand pounds on the wrong component order.

ME

Written by Going Eagle Editorial Team · Reviewed by Mike Evans, Golf Coach — 6 years of coaching experience

Build in the right order

The most common expensive mistake is buying the launch monitor first, then discovering the room cannot accommodate it. Space constrains everything, so measure before you shop.

  1. Measure the space. Ceiling height first — it decides whether you can swing a driver at all. Then width, then depth.
  2. Choose the launch monitor. Camera-based units generally need space behind the ball; radar units generally need space in front. The room decides this, not the review scores.
  3. Buy the best hitting mat you can afford. This is the component people under-spend on, and a hard mat punishes your wrists and elbows over time.
  4. Then screen, enclosure, projector and software, in whatever order the budget allows.

The costs that surprise people

Budget for the things that never appear in a headline package price: a short-throw projector if the room is tight, a computer capable of running the software, lighting consistent enough for a camera-based unit, and flooring or side netting to protect the room. On a mid-range build these routinely add a third to the total.

Measured versus estimated data

This distinction matters more than any brand comparison. Every launch monitor measures some values and calculates the rest from a model. A unit that measures ball speed and estimates spin will look confident and be wrong in exactly the situations where you most need it to be right. Before comparing prices, find out which metrics each unit actually measures.
