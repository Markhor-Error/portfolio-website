Swing Fixes

Golf Swing Fixes: Diagnose the Miss Before You Change Anything

Almost every golfer tries to fix a bad shot before they have correctly identified what the bad shot is. Start here instead: name the miss, understand what it says about impact, then change one thing.

ME

Written by Going Eagle Editorial Team · Reviewed by Mike Evans, Golf Coach — 6 years of coaching experience

Fix the cause, not the symptom

Two golfers can hit an identical-looking slice for opposite reasons. One has a face that will not square. The other has a face that is fine and a path that swings hard across the ball. Give them the same tip and you help one and ruin the other.

This is why every guide in this section starts from impact conditions rather than from a drill. Under the modern ball flight laws, the ball starts close to where the face points, and it curves according to the difference between the face and the path. Those two facts explain every shot you have ever hit, and they tell you which of your habits is actually responsible.

Two minutes well spent

The ball flight diagnostic asks where your shot starts and which way it bends, then tells you what that combination means at impact. It is free, and it will stop you working on the wrong thing.

The order to work in

When a lesson gets expensive, it is usually because the golfer started at step four. Work through these in order and most faults resolve before you reach the swing itself.

  1. Alignment. Lay a club down and check where you are genuinely aimed. Almost nobody aims where they think they do, and a surprising share of swing faults are compensations for bad aim.
  2. Grip. The single biggest influence on whether the face can return to square without manipulation.
  3. Ball position and posture. Cheap to change, and responsible for a large share of fat, thin and topped shots.
  4. Path and pivot. Only once the first three are stable is it worth changing how the club travels.
